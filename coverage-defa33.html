coverage

a composition taken apart, sent through the air one part at a time, caught across the coverage area, and put back together from what came back.

the statement

coverage is a composition assembled from recordings gathered at broadcast points across the catskills. before the residency i will compose the eventual structure of the track, lengths, parts, loops, samples etc all laid out according to a coherent song structure - during the residency each part goes out on wgxc 90.7-fm in bursts of thirty to ninety seconds on a fixed schedule, and a ring of receivers left at listening posts across the coverage area, a ridge, a valley floor, the edge of the signal, catches every burst. we catalog every received transmission.

each day is built from the one before. day one sends the raw parts. day two sends combinations made only from day one's receptions, and so on, and the station's processing and the terrain have written themselves into all of it. to listeners the first days sound like scattered fragments; day by day they cohere, until the station is playing coherent parts of a composition. the residency culminates in an evening transmission of the full piece, built entirely from received parts.

the library of receptions is then published as an online instrument. listeners build their own version of the piece by choosing, part by part, which post's reception to use. This generates an essentially infinite pool of combinations to restructure - do you want a fully mangled track from the edge of reception? no problem. Want to hear something close to the original prepared composition? you can do that too.

a recurring broadcast

the parts go out on a fixed interval, every hour or two as the schedule allows. each burst thirty to ninety seconds. a ring of listening posts catches all of them. recorders set at the art park. a ridge above the valley, a hollow behind it, the last place the signal still reads. and, if the station's listeners will have them, a few windowsills. every part is heard from every place, every day. each day we pull the cards and catalog them into the broadcast library.

for the station's listeners, the first days are a minute of something on the hour: a drum loop, then a pad, then a sequence, each caught somewhere new. it will sound like sampling. by mid-week the bursts are combinations, and by the weekend the hour carries something pretty close to a piece of music.

the days

day 1the raw parts go out, one per burst, on rotation. every post catches every one.
day 2combinations built only from day one's receptions. the pad from the ridge and the drum loop from the hollow become one layer. the sequence from the valley floor goes out on its own again.
days 3–5variations. layers recombine, swap places, thin and thicken, always made from the previous day's receptions and never from the original files. by day five every part has been through the air several times from several places, and the bursts have started to sound like a piece.
days 6–8the assembly. the full composition is built from the library: for each part, which day, which post.
days 9–10the evening transmission. then the documentation: the log of positions, the wear scores, the library catalogued.

the culminating event is a live evening transmission of the full composition on wgxc 90.7-fm and wavefarm.org/listen, from the acra studio, assembled entirely from received parts. no loudspeakers. the only way to hear it in the art park is through a receiver: the audience walks with handheld radios, and each hears a different piece, a different fade, a different neighbour bleeding in, depending on where they stand. they control what version of the final composition they want to experience.

why radio?

it is a portrait of the station. every part passes through wgxc's own studio-to-transmitter link, its processor, its tower and its coverage area. by the end of the week the station's signal chain is written into every layer of the piece. a plugin could model a channel. it cannot be wgxc.

the wear is public. the parts are made on air, on a schedule, on a frequency anyone can tune to. everyone in range hears the piece being built over a few days of transmissions.

the wear is made of the place. the same part caught at every post on the same hour is a dozen different parts. the composition is assembled by choosing among places. terrain, weather and time of day are in the mix decisions.

the lineage is alvin lucier's i am sitting in a room, a voice re-recorded through a room until only the room remains; william basinski's the disintegration loops, tape played until the oxide is gone; and wave farm's own free103point9 years, when the station was a microradio collective. coverage takes the structure of the first, the material of the second and the medium of the third.

the library

every burst at every post is kept and catalogued: which part, which day, which generation, which post, what time, what weather. it is a grid: parts by days by places. by the end of the week that is potentially a few thousand short recordings, each one the same composition heard from a different place at a different age. the finished piece is one path through the grid. the library is the work as much as the transmission is.

after the residency the library is published as an online instrument on newspeechsound.com. the composition is laid out part by part, and for each part the listener chooses a cell of the grid: which day, which post, and so how much wear. keep the melody clean and let the drums disintegrate. take everything from the ridge. use only day five. every version is the same piece and every sound in it has been through the air over the catskills.

about newspeech

newspeech is chris elkjar's audio and video creative output: a band, a software sequencer, a set of destruction and loop-wear tools, a 24/7 generative broadcast programme, and some records made with them. the practice is built around the concept of unsynced systems intersecting and destruction, noise and wear as material.

  • broadcast — the studio's autonomous set programme: idents, interstitials, standby, a picture that follows the display.
  • decay — digital disintegration loops: a wear map applied to a loop on every pass. the instrument for this piece starts here.
  • drone, glitch, slice — free browser tools
  • news — the record of the work.
