sequence

sixteen rows — eight rhythm, eight melody — up to 64 steps each. every step carries its own pitch, velocity, chance, ratchet, timing and gate.

the first melody row is the chord master: pick chords by degree and extension, and it publishes them to the rest. the second is the bass. the others follow the chord, walk the scale, drone on the root, or ignore it all.

  • change root or scale mid-play and everything reharmonizes
  • per-row length, euclidean hits, rotation and step rate
  • per-step chord overrides, slice choice and an accumulator

mangle

every row has a mutation dial: velocity jitter, pitch jumps, gate wobble, ties flipping. on the chord master it drops tones, borrows chords from the parallel mode and reshuffles voicings.

six macros move the whole rig at once — density, motion, drift, chaos, tension, voicing. switch on auto and the ghost walks the patterns by itself, morphing the macros and playing fills into each change.

  • the grid keeps what you wrote; the roll tab shows what is actually sounding
  • freeze holds the current state
  • a timing lock per row keeps the rhythm and mutates only the notes

polish

every voice is a sample, played four ways: sample, slice, wavetable or granular. each has trim, tuning, a filter, drive and bit-crush, plus an lfo or envelope per target.

the send chain is a tape stage, glitch, reverb and a synced delay. the loop unit grabs the last 1–8 bars of the mix and plays them back at any speed with a granular layer on top. the noise unit is clocked digital noise through a wasp-style filter.

  • a compressor, saturator and eq on every track
  • four global lfos, routable to any knob
  • a master chain with compressor, distortion and inflate

arrange

pattern pads snapshot and recall the whole rack, bar-quantized. scene pads do the same one level up. the song view arranges them: rows of scene, pattern and bar count, each with its own track mutes, played top to bottom.

  • loop a section, drag rows to reorder
  • a next-pattern cue with a count through the last bar
  • perform pads for live punch-ins: repeat, filter, bits, scrub, chop, reverse

record

arm rec and press play. one mixed wav, or a full stems folder: master, the fx, reverb and delay buses, and a wav per track. recording keeps going after stop until the tails ring out.

  • a .mid beside every stem and for every hardware synth
  • export a whole song as midi without playing it
  • export any voice as a .pti for a polyend tracker

control

any row can play an external synth over midi instead of a sample. sequence runs as clock master or follower, and records notes in from a keyboard, snapped to the scale.

  • bind any hardware cc to any knob or pad with learn mode
  • a launchpad in programmer mode edits the grid directly
  • a launch control xl3 preset gives a full pre-built surface

also in there

edit modes

notes, velocity, chance, ratchet, timing, gate — keys 1–6.

ties

shift-click ties a run of steps on melodic rows.

slicing

auto or grid slices, with a slice per step or random per hit.

accumulator

a per-step climbing ladder: wrap, bounce or hold.

kits

any folder of wavs is a kit. note-named files map themselves.

loop to kit

bounce the loop unit into the library as a new voice.

count-in + metronome

a one-bar count and a click that never reaches the recording.

output routing

per-row outputs on a multi-channel interface.

lfo routing

select an lfo, click a knob. grab the knob to override it.

.seq files

songs save as plain json.

install

open the dmg, drag sequence to applications.

on first run macos asks for microphone access. that is how the app lists audio devices — say yes.

samples

the app reads kits from ~/documents/sequence/samples, sorted into drums/ bass/ instruments/ pads/ textures/ breaks/ loops/ — one folder of wavs per kit.

grab kits from newspeech samples and unzip each into its category folder. to use another folder: settings → user samples → change…
